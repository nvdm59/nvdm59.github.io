Navid Mehrdad

Political scientist

published before 2020 as Navid Hassanpour

I study how electoral institutions are born: why the first age of mass politics, from the 1860s to the 1930s, produced durable democracies in some polities and durable dictatorships in others. I hold Ph.D.s in political science from Yale (2014) and in electrical engineering from Stanford (2006), and have held research and teaching positions at Princeton (Niehaus Fellow in Regional Political Economy), Columbia, the Higher School of Economics in Moscow (Associate Professor of Political Science), the European University Institute, and Yale (Henry Hart Rice Faculty Fellow). The research behind my current book began in the parliamentary and state archives of Tehran, St. Petersburg and Moscow, Istanbul, and Beijing, and draws on sources in eight languages.

Political Origins of Dictatorship and Democracy (book manuscript, 2026)

Elite Entrepreneurs in the First Age of Modern Mass Politics — complete manuscript, under review with university presses.

Across the first wave, suffrage was extended, restricted, and redesigned by elite political entrepreneurs competing with one another, and the extensions came from strength, not weakness: incumbents extended the franchise when they expected the new voters to make elections more favorable to themselves. A constituency-level test across twenty-five countries (395 elections, roughly 52,000 district-elections) supports the prediction, and seven case studies, from Bismarck’s Germany and Meiji-Taishō Japan to Romanov Russia, the Ottoman state, Qajar Iran, and Republican China, document the calculation in the decision-makers’ own words. The fate of each regime was then decided in the confrontations that followed with the mass organizations electoral politics called into being: absorption and electoral democracy, exclusion and revolution, or a monopolized arena and the modern durable dictatorship.

The book rests on datasets built from primary material: 2,951 district-level results from twenty-four Iranian Majlis elections (1909–1975); the court-commissioned record of Russia’s 1906 elections and a 1,698-uezd dataset of the 1917 Constituent Assembly returns; a deputy-level roster of the Ottoman chambers of 1908, 1912, and 1914; the franchise decisions of the German federal states; and a panel of Southern U.S. district-elections around the disfranchising measures of 1890–1908. A full replication archive accompanies the text. The table of contents, chapter abstracts, and proposal are available on request.

Leading from the Periphery and Network Collective Action (Cambridge University Press, 2017)

My first book argues for a reformulation of the collective action dilemma based on decentralized leadership: mobilization that begins at the margins of a network, against theories built around central leaders and unifying themes. It received the American Political Science Association Political Networks Section’s best book award (2015–17) and was published under the name Navid Hassanpour. Cambridge UP
